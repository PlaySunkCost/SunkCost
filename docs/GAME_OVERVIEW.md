# Sunk Cost — the game in two pages

A 1–4 player online co-op salvage horror game (PC, Steam). First person. Stylised, chunky,
low-poly look in the R.E.P.O. / PEAK direction — not photorealistic. Played completely straight:
the game is never funny on purpose; every laugh comes from physics, panic and your friends.

## The premise

Around the year 2300, on an ocean world that is not Earth, a crew of futuristic pirates — raiders and
scavengers, not a corporation — owns you. You work off a debt you will never clear. Their ship, the
**Black Tide Salvage**, lowers you in a glass elevator to the seafloor, where you walk in the dark, find
treasure, and drag it back to the elevator. Every time the elevator rises, it screams your position
across the water.

## The world below

The seafloor holds a failed human colony built over much older alien ruins. Shallow sites are colony
housing and wrecked ships; deeper sites get less and less human until nothing makes sense. It is never
explained. Loot is a mix of recognisable human junk (volume, comedy) and alien artifacts (high value,
high fear). Four sites at launch; each dive the site reloads fresh, with 0–2 random conditions
(blackout, silt storm, infestation… occasionally good ones), revealed only afterwards.

## The loop

Three places:
1. **HQ** — the pirates' offshore platform, where the ship docks. Pay the quota, buy gear at the shop,
   play basketball on the court, and save. Only here.
2. **The ship at sea** — home during a cycle. A navigation console chooses where to sail; a TV shows the
   divers below; a storage room fills with everything brought up.
3. **The dive site** — reached only by the glass elevator. It floods as it goes under the sea and drains
   on the way up.

A **cycle is three days**; a day is one dive. At the end of a cycle the crew sails home and hands
everything over at HQ. The quota must be met, and it climbs every cycle. Short on payday: the whole
crew **walks the plank** — the run is over, and everything starts from nothing. The crew can also vote
unanimously to give up. Runs are endless; the aim is to last as many cycles as you can. Between runs
you keep only cosmetics.

## Diving

- **Walking only** (no swimming), one **air tank** each (12–15 minutes; bigger tanks to buy), no refills
  below except carried air items. Sprinting burns air faster. A **dash** dodges.
- **Health** doesn't regenerate. Attacks can open a **leak** (air drains 3×) that a teammate or a patch
  kit must seal.
- Carry 4 slots plus one hand; everything has weight and slows you; big objects take both hands.
- **Everything makes noise** — footsteps, landings, thrown things, the elevator's scream — and some
  monsters hunt by sound. A distinct lamp colour per player is how you tell who a shape in the dark is.
- **Death costs everything you bought** unless your crew hauls your **body** back up: a corpse is cargo
  competing with the treasure for your hands. The dead watch as spectators. If the whole crew dies,
  "NOBODY CAME BACK", and the day ends.

## The monsters (you can't kill anything)

No weapons. You avoid, distract and outlast. Three roam each dive, drawn from:
- **Long Walker** — sees you, then walks after you, slowly, forever.
- **Weeping Angel** — moves only when nobody is looking at it.
- **Charger** — shakes, then rushes in a straight line; step aside.
- **Lure** — drawn to your lit headlamp; burns a beam of light at you.
- **Listener** — blind; hears everything and shoots at the sound.
- **Impostor** — only one diver sees it, wearing a crewmate's colour and name.
- **Elevator Ghost** — sometimes, when the car comes back down for someone, it isn't empty.

The deck and the elevator are always safe: nothing comes above water or into the car.

## The feeling it aims for

Four friends on voice chat, greedy and scared, arguing whether to carry the gold or the body. The test
that matters: strangers playing it say out loud, unprompted, the things the game is built to make them
say — the greed, the panic, the blame and the laugh.
